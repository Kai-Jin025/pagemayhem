If you are searching for an "unblocked" Page Rage, here is the honest lay of the land — what actually works for this game, what doesn't exist, and which shortcuts are traps.

First, the thing nobody tells you

"Unblocked" games usually means one thing: a game hosted on a portal got blocked, and a mirror site on a fresh domain hasn't been blocked yet. That model needs a portal game with copies floating around. Page Rage is not that. It is a game that lives at its own home, page-rage.com, plus an official browser extension — and there is no second copy anywhere, because only the game's own developers have the right to host it. If your network blocks the site, the game goes with it, and no legitimate "unblocked mirror" exists to run to. What you have instead are real options, in increasing order of how much we can recommend them.

Option 2: The extension route — Page Rage's special move for restricted networks

The add-on route is the cleverest legitimate workaround in the genre, and it is where Page Rage has an edge: its official extension works on the page already in your browser, so once it is installed you never need to visit a game website at all. If the filter blocks game domains but leaves your browser's add-on store alone, the extension is the one official door still open — a game that sidesteps the block without anyone bypassing anything. The catches are real, though: installing needs the official store to be reachable and allowed on your device, and managed school devices (Chromebooks especially) usually forbid extensions outright. Option 4: The options we do not recommend (and why)

  • Third-party "unblocked" mirror sites. Sites that rehost someone else's game under a fresh domain are the sketchiest corner of this genre — malware, redirect chains, fake play buttons and phishing pages are routine. For this game they have an extra problem: there is no legitimate source to copy from, because the developers have never released a rehostable build. Anything claiming to be a "Page Rage mirror" is either a copy nobody authorized or something worse.
  • "Unblocked extension" files from outside the official stores. A repackaged extension is riskier than a web mirror, not safer: whatever is inside it runs directly in your browser, on every page you visit. The developers' listing in the Chrome Web Store, Firefox Add-ons or Edge Add-ons is the only copy they published — anywhere else, you have no idea what you are installing.
  • Proxy and VPN services on a school network. Whether these are acceptable is a question for your school's rules, not for us — but understand that many schools treat bypassing the filter as a far more serious violation than the game itself. Losing network access over a browser toy is a bad trade.
  • Anything that asks you to sign in "to play." No game in this genre has ever needed your account. That is a phishing page, full stop.

We don't recommend them. That depends on your school's acceptable-use rules, not on us. Many schools treat proxy and VPN use as a far more serious violation than the game itself. On a network you don't own, follow the rules that apply to you — Page Rage will still be there at home.
