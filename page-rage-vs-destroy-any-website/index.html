Page Rage vs Destroy Any Website: Which Should You Play?

The genre has two flagships: the original viral hit and the smooth physics rival. Here is the full comparison — weapons, multiplayer, the extension trick — plus a ten-second test for which one fits you.

The ten-second answer: want to fly, and want to destroy the exact page you are looking at in one click? Play Page Rage. Want the bigger arsenal and friends demolishing the same page with you? Play Destroy Any Website. Both are free — playing both is also a valid answer.

The short version

Both games perform the same magic trick: hand them a web page and they hand you back that page as a destructible level — real layout, real headlines, rebuilt block by pixelated block inside the game. The real website is never touched; the destruction happens on a local copy, which is why the whole genre is harmless theater rather than hacking. Once you have seen the family resemblance, the differences start at the mood: Destroy Any Website is a punchy, retro-arcade shooter starring a stickman with an ever-bigger gun, while Page Rage is a smoother physics sandbox where you fly around on a jetpack and take the page apart at your own pace. Both are free, both run in a normal browser tab, and the choice between them comes down to three things: the feel, the multiplayer, and one workflow feature covered below.

Weapons: a toolkit vs a spectacle

Page Rage gives you around ten weapons and lets you swap between them freely with the number keys — small precise tools for picking apart a navigation bar, heavy ones for toppling entire sections — and the jetpack changes the angles you can attack from, since you can hover above a page and drop destruction on it. Destroy Any Website makes the arsenal part of the show: you start with a pistol, and the game keeps handing you bigger and more absurd hardware as the page comes apart — submachine gun, shotgun, rocket launcher, rail gun, flamethrower, and eventually weapons best described as "too much." A destruction meter climbs toward 100% as you flatten blocks, which gives the rampage a finish line that Page Rage deliberately does not have. Page Rage keeps score by vibes; Destroy Any Website keeps score by percentage.

Playing together — or alone

This one is easy: if playing with friends matters, Destroy Any Website is the clear pick. It has online rooms, and a group of up to about half a dozen players can pile into the same web page and demolish it together — the last few intact blocks turn into a miniature team sport. Page Rage is a solo sandbox: you, the jetpack, and the page. Neither approach is a flaw — some sessions you want company, some you want quiet — but if your dream session is a group tearing apart one article at once, that is Destroy Any Website's territory.

The extension is Page Rage's trump card

Adopting a page into these games usually takes four steps: copy the URL, switch tabs, paste, wait. Page Rage's official extension deletes all four. A button sits in your toolbar, and one click opens the game with the page you are already looking at rebuilt as your level — no URL handling at all. It is available for Chrome, Firefox and Edge, and it is the single biggest workflow gap between the two games: Destroy Any Website has no extension, so pasting URLs remains its way in, which is fine but feels like dial-up once you have tried the button. So which should you pick?

  • You want the fastest route from "annoying site" to "rubble": Page Rage with its extension.
  • You want bigger explosions and a finish line: Destroy Any Website's absurd arsenal and destruction meter.
  • You want to play with friends: Destroy Any Website's online rooms.
  • You can't decide: perfect — play both. They are free, they are siblings, and the genre's whole appeal is thirty seconds of catharsis on demand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Page Rage better than Destroy Any Website?

They are siblings rather than clones. Page Rage is the smoother physics sandbox — jetpack, around ten weapons, six built-in pages and the any-tab browser extension. Destroy Any Website is the punchier arcade shooter with the escalating arsenal, a destruction meter and online multiplayer rooms. If you enjoy one, you will almost certainly enjoy the other.

Which of the two has multiplayer?

Destroy Any Website — it has online rooms where up to about half a dozen players demolish the same page together. Page Rage is a solo sandbox, so for group demolition you want the original.

Which one works as a browser extension?

Page Rage. Its official extension for Chrome, Firefox and Edge puts a Break This Page button in your toolbar so any tab becomes a level in one click. Destroy Any Website has no extension — its workflow is pasting a URL.

Are Page Rage and Destroy Any Website both free?

Yes — both are free browser games with no download and no account. Page Rage lives at page-rage.com; Destroy Any Website lives on its developer's site, alongside his other tools.

Can I play them on a phone?

Both are built around keyboard and mouse, so a desktop browser gives the real experience. Destroy Any Website is officially desktop-only; Page Rage will load on a phone or tablet, but flying the jetpack without a mouse is much harder.
