Games Like Destroy Any Website

You flattened a few pages, the novelty is settling in, and now you want more. Here is every alternative worth your time — browser games, add-ons, the genre's ancestors, and one paid game for when you want destruction in high definition.

1. Page Rage — the closest match

If you want "more Destroy Any Website," this is the answer. Page Rage takes the same core idea — paste a page, smash the page — and rebuilds it as a smoother physics sandbox with a jetpack, around ten weapons and a set of built-in destructible levels. Its official Chrome/Firefox/Edge extension is the genre's best convenience feature: click it on any site and that exact page opens as your level. Where Destroy Any Website is a punchy arcade shooter, Page Rage is a floaty playground, and the two complement each other perfectly.

2. Site Slayer — destruction where you stand

Site Slayer is the third member of the big three: a Firefox/Edge add-on that tears apart the page you are currently on, without any URL pasting at all. It is also the most "game" of the bunch — several destruction modes, a fleet of ships, power-ups, leaderboards and 50+ achievements give it the kind of progression that the others deliberately skip. Play it if catharsis alone isn't enough and you want a reason to come back tomorrow.

3. Stimulation Clicker — chaos of a different flavor

Neal Agarwal's Stimulation Clicker is not about destroying a specific page, but it belongs on this list because it delivers the same sensory overload from a different angle: popups multiplying into infinity, buttons begging to be pressed, a screen dissolving into glorious nonsense. If the part of Destroy Any Website you loved was the escalating chaos rather than the demolition specifically, this is your next stop.

4. Desktop Destroyer — the genre's grandfather

Long before anyone thought to smash a website, Desktop Destroyer let a generation of office workers spend their breaks hitting their own desktop icons with a hammer, a gun, a flamethrower and a swarm of insects. It's crude by modern standards, gloriously nostalgic, and free to find (it's classic abandonware), and it proves the itch this whole genre scratches has been with us for decades.

5. Teardown — destruction as a full-time job (paid, PC)

If you want the concept at console scale, Teardown is a paid PC game about planning and executing fully simulated heists and demolitions in a voxel world. It is not a browser toy and it is not free — but it is the deepest expression of "everything in this world can come apart" that exists, and genre fans graduate to it eventually.

6. ...and the next viral one

Web page destruction went from a single viral game to a genre in about a week in late September 2026, with clones, extensions and imitators appearing constantly. Some are genuinely good — the ones above — and many are quick cash-ins. The safest filter: stick to games with a real developer behind them, avoid anything that asks for a download when the genre is all browser games, and never trust a "destruction" site that asks for your login. Frequently asked questions

What is the closest game to Destroy Any Website?

Page Rage is the closest match — same core idea, smoother physics, a jetpack instead of an arcade feel, and a browser extension that loads any page straight into the game.

Are these alternatives free?

Page Rage, Site Slayer and Stimulation Clicker are free. Desktop Destroyer is free abandonware. Teardown is the one paid entry on the list, and it is a full PC game rather than a browser toy.

Is there a game that destroys real websites instead of copies?

No — and you should be deeply suspicious of anything advertising that. Every legitimate game in this genre rebuilds the page inside its own canvas. A copy gets destroyed; the real site never changes.

Which alternative has multiplayer?

Destroy Any Website itself has online rooms for small groups. Among the alternatives, Page Rage and Site Slayer are single-player experiences, so for group demolition you will want the original.

Will new games like these keep appearing?

Almost certainly. Web page destruction only became a genre in late September 2026, and new takes are appearing constantly. This page is updated as the scene grows, so check back.
