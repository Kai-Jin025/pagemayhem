Web Page Destruction Games

The browser games that turn any website into a smashable playground — the full lineup, what each one does, and where to start.

Somewhere in late September 2026, the internet discovered that pasting a URL into a little browser game and then taking a rocket launcher to the resulting pixel city is deeply, unreasonably satisfying. The clip spread, the news write-ups followed, and within about a week the genre had grown from one viral hit into a small family of games with different styles, different physics and different ideas of what "destruction" means. This page tracks all of them in one place: what each game is, how it plays, and which one you should open first.

Destroy Any Website The original

The one that started the wave. Paste any URL, and the page reappears as a retro, 90s-arcade world where a stickman hero blasts every element apart with an escalating arsenal — pistol to railgun to flamethrower — while a destruction meter ticks toward 100%. Has online multiplayer rooms, so up to half a dozen players can demolish the same page together. Made by French developer Hugo Duprez and free in your browser.

Page Rage Smoothest physics

The closest rival, with a jetpack, ten weapons and a cleaner, sandbox feel. It ships six built-in destructible pages so you can play instantly, and it also exists as a Chrome, Firefox and Edge extension: click "Break this page" on any real site and it opens that exact page in the game. If Destroy Any Website is the arcade cabinet, Page Rage is the physics playground.

Site Slayer One click

The laziest — in a good way — way to play: a Firefox and Edge add-on that turns the page you are already staring at into a destruction playground on the spot. Six destruction modes, a fleet of 13 ships, power-ups, leaderboards and dozens of achievements give it the most "game" structure of the three.

How a web page becomes a playground

  1. You pick a victim. Every game in this genre starts the same way: you paste a URL, or you use a button that grabs the page you are on. Some games also ship a few built-in pages for instant play.
  2. The game rebuilds it as pixels. The page is fetched, sliced into blocks and reassembled as a little destructible world inside the game canvas. Text becomes platforms, images become debris, banners become things to knock over. The layout you know is still recognizable — which is exactly why smashing it feels personal.
  3. You go to work. From there the games diverge: some give you guns and a destruction meter to fill, some give you a jetpack and a physics sandbox, some let you choose from several destruction modes and just let you loose. There is no score to chase in most of them, and no losing — the point is the catharsis.

Why it feels so good (and why it's harmless)

Web page destruction games hit the same nerve as bubble wrap, sandbox games and that one browser classic where you throw a yeti — with one extra ingredient: the thing you are demolishing looks like a real website. News homepages, office software, the site that logged you out at the worst moment. It's the digital equivalent of punching a pillow with someone's face on it, and researchers have long noted that playful, consequence-free aggression is a pressure valve, not a problem.

The "consequence-free" part is real. These games rebuild a copy of the page inside your browser; the actual website is never touched, never slow, never even aware. Nothing is sent to the site you destroy, no one is hacking anyone, and a login-protected page can't be reached at all. It is theater — and that's the point.

Frequently asked questions

Are web page destruction games free?

Yes — Destroy Any Website, Page Rage and Site Slayer are all free to play. None of them asks for a download or an account.

Do these games actually destroy the website?

No. The game takes the page you paste and rebuilds it as a pixel playground inside your browser. The real website is never modified, and the people who run it never notice. It is pure harmless catharsis.

Why is destroying websites suddenly everywhere?

Destroy Any Website went viral in late September 2026 — news sites in a dozen countries wrote it up — and a small genre grew around it overnight. If you saw a clip of a stickman blasting apart a news homepage, this is that family of games.

Which web page destruction game is best?

Destroy Any Website has the biggest arsenal and online multiplayer; Page Rage feels smoother and comes as a browser extension; Site Slayer turns a tab into a playground in one click. Read the guides below and start with the one that fits how you want to play.

Do they work on a Chromebook or school laptop?

They are browser games, so any modern browser can run them with no install. Site Slayer is an extension, so it needs your network to allow add-ons. Performance depends on the page you load — media-heavy pages get choppier.

Can you really destroy any website?

Most URLs work — the game fetches the page and rebuilds it as blocks you can smash. A few sites refuse to be embedded or are too heavy, and pages behind a login cannot be reached. Try a couple of your favorites.
