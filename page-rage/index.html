Page Rage: Jetpack, Weapons & the Break-This-Page Extension

If Destroy Any Website is a 90s arcade cabinet, Page Rage is the physics sandbox next to it — and its browser extension puts every tab of your browser one click away from being demolished.

What Page Rage is

Page Rage is a free browser game built on the same irresistible premise as the rest of the genre: hand it a web page and it hands you back a destructible playground version of that page, ready to be flattened. Where the original viral hit went for retro arcade chaos, Page Rage goes for smooth, fluid destruction — you fly around the page with a jetpack, pick from a double-digit arsenal, and knock apart the layout at your own pace. It lives at page-rage.com and, like its rivals, runs entirely in your browser with no download and no account.

What makes it stand out in a suddenly crowded genre is convenience. Page Rage ships with a set of built-in destructible pages, so there is always something to wreck instantly, and — more importantly — it also exists as an official browser extension for Chrome, Firefox and Edge. Install it, and a small button appears in your toolbar. Land on any website, click the button, and the game opens with that exact page as your level. No URL copying, no tab juggling. It is the fastest known route from "this page annoys me" to "this page is rubble."

Play it now

Open page-rage.com in your desktop browser, pick one of the built-in pages, and start flying. If you like what you feel, grab the extension from your browser's add-on store (it's listed for Chrome, Firefox and Edge) and take it site-hopping.

Fair warning: once the extension is installed, no website is safe. That is the entire point.

How to play

  1. Choose a level. Use one of the built-in pages, or type in any URL if you want a specific victim. Heavy media sites load chunkier than simple text pages — same rule as every game in the genre.
  2. Fly first, fight second. The jetpack changes the whole feel. Spend your first minute just moving around: hovering above the page gives you a much better read of where the fragile, load-bearing pieces are.
  3. Switch weapons as the layout demands. The game gives you around ten tools; the number keys swap between them. Small precise weapons for picking apart navigation and text blocks, big explosive ones for toppling entire sections. Demolition is a rhythm — find the one that matches the page.
  4. Restart or reload whenever. The page can be rebuilt for another pass, and you can jump to a new URL without losing your flow. Nothing is scored, nothing is saved — it's a toy, not a campaign.

Exact key bindings are shown in the in-game tutorial on first load; the game is keyboard-and-mouse by design, so desktop browsers play best.

The extension is the killer feature

Adopting a page into a destruction game usually means copying the URL, switching tabs, pasting, waiting. Page Rage's extension deletes all four steps. Whenever a website earns your rage — an unskippable popup, a checkout that forgot your cart, a "we value your privacy" wall that values nothing — you click the toolbar button and the game opens pre-loaded with that page. The entire workflow is: rage, click, wreck. It works with the game's desktop experience and is the single most convincing reason to choose Page Rage as your daily driver in this genre.

Page Rage vs Destroy Any Website: which to pick?

They are siblings, not clones, and the differences are mostly about mood:

  • Physics feel: Page Rage is smoother and floatier thanks to the jetpack; Destroy Any Website feels punchier and more arcade.
  • Convenience: Page Rage wins outright with its browser extension and built-in pages.
  • Multiplayer: Destroy Any Website has online rooms for group demolition; Page Rage is primarily a solo sandbox.
  • The vibe: retro arcade chaos versus modern physics playground. Neither is better — pick by mood, switch freely.

If you want the full lineup of games in this style, the genre hub compares everything in one place, and the Destroy Any Website guide covers the original in depth.

Five habits that make destruction more fun

  • Start high, work down. With a jetpack you can attack from above; collapsed upper sections take the lower ones with them.
  • Clear the edges last. Corners and margins survive the longest and give you a satisfying cleanup phase — or a reason to replay.
  • Pick pages with emotional weight. Your email provider. Your bank. The site of a game you rage-quit. The genre runs on recognition.
  • Use light pages for speedruns. Simple blogs and wikis can be fully flattened in under a minute; competing against your own record is the only score that exists here.
  • Keep it theatrical. Nothing you smash is real, the real site never notices, and nothing is sent anywhere — it's a pantomime of destruction. That's what makes it safe to enjoy wherever you legitimately play.

Frequently asked questions

Is Page Rage free to play?

Yes. The game runs free at page-rage.com, and the browser extension is also free — there is nothing to buy and no account to create.

What does the Page Rage extension do?

It adds a button to your browser toolbar. Click it on any page and the game opens with that exact page loaded as a destructible level — no copy-pasting URLs.

What is the difference between Page Rage and Destroy Any Website?

Both turn a web page into a smashable level. Page Rage leans into a smoother physics sandbox with a jetpack and several built-in pages, while Destroy Any Website is more of an arcade shooter with multiplayer rooms. Try both — they scratch the same itch differently.

Does Page Rage have built-in levels?

Yes — it ships with a set of built-in destructible pages, including classics of the genre, so you can play instantly without typing a URL first.

Can I play Page Rage on mobile?

The game is designed around keyboard and mouse, so desktop browsers give the best experience. It will load on a phone or tablet, but aiming and flying are much harder without a mouse.
